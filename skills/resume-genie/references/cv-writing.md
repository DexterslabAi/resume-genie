# Writing the tailored CV

How Resume Genie rewrites a CV for one job while keeping it 100% truthful.

## CV tailoring

Tailoring is editing, not inventing. Treat the candidate's history as a library. For each job you choose which books go on the front shelf.

### Order of work
1. **Headline.** One line under the name that names the target role and two or three of the candidate's strongest relevant themes. For example: "Finance Automation Product Lead | P2P and R2R | SAP S/4HANA".
2. **Summary.** 3 or 4 sentences: who they are in the employer's terms, years and domain, one or two signature results, and what they bring to *this* role. No "results-driven professional" or "passionate team player".
3. **Skills.** Group them into 2 to 4 labelled clusters, with the most relevant cluster first. Use the advert's terms. Drop anything irrelevant to this role.
4. **Experience.** Keep every role, with dates unchanged. Within each role:
   - Put the bullets most relevant to this job first.
   - Rewrite bullets so their language matches the advert, while describing the same real work.
   - Give recent and relevant roles 4 to 6 bullets. Compress older or unrelated ones to 1 or 2.
5. **Education and certifications.** Keep them short. Bring forward certifications the advert names.

### What may change
Order, emphasis, wording, grouping and which details are included.

### What must not change
Employers, titles, dates, qualifications, tools used and results achieved. A job title may be clarified only in brackets and only when it is true. For example: "Analyst III (Senior Process Analyst)".

### Log every change
For each meaningful edit, record the section, what changed and why it helps for this job. The candidate must be able to review and reject any edit.

## Achievement bullets

### The shape
**Strong verb + what you did + how or at what scale + the result.**

Weak: "Responsible for invoice processing improvements."
Strong: "Redesigned the invoice approval flow across 6 countries, cutting processing time from 9 to 4 days."

The result can come before the method when it is the most impressive part: "Cut month-end close from 8 to 5 days by automating 14 reconciliations."

### Rules
- Start with a past-tense action verb (present tense for the current role is fine if used consistently). Never start with "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped with" or "Duties included".
- Use one idea per bullet, in 1 or 2 lines (roughly 15 to 30 words).
- Be specific: name the system, process, audience or market.
- Show the outcome: time saved, cost reduced, revenue, quality, risk reduced, adoption, or a decision it enabled.
- Vary the verbs. Don't use "Led" five times in one role.

### Verb bank by theme
- **Leading:** led, directed, headed, mobilised, chaired
- **Building:** designed, built, launched, introduced, established
- **Improving:** streamlined, automated, redesigned, simplified, standardised
- **Persuading:** secured, negotiated, won, aligned, influenced
- **Analysing:** diagnosed, modelled, forecast, quantified, audited
- **Delivering:** delivered, rolled out, migrated, implemented, shipped

### When there is no result in the CV
Describe the scope truthfully (who, how many, how often) and leave a bracketed placeholder for the missing number, such as "[X%]" or "[N] users". Never guess. See the metrics playbook.

## Metrics without fabrication

Numbers make a CV believable, but an invented number is a lie that can surface in an interview, a reference check or a background screen. Resume Genie's rule: **find the opportunity, never fill it with a guess.**

### Where numbers usually hide
- **Scale:** team size, number of countries, sites, users, entities, suppliers, transactions or invoices per month, budget managed.
- **Speed:** cycle time before and after, days to close, time to hire, delivery ahead of schedule.
- **Money:** savings, cost avoided, revenue influenced, working capital released.
- **Quality:** error rate, first-time-right, audit findings, SLA attainment, customer satisfaction.
- **Adoption:** percentage of users or processes moved to the new way.

### How to handle each bullet
1. If the CV or the candidate's notes contain the number, use it exactly as given.
2. You may derive figures by simple arithmetic from numbers the candidate stated. For example, 9 days down to 4 days is a 56% reduction.
3. If a number would clearly help but isn't given, write a placeholder in square brackets that says what is needed: "[N] analysts", "[X%] fewer exceptions", "[£X] annual savings". Mark the bullet needs_confirmation = true.
4. Use no more than one or two placeholders per bullet. If a bullet would need three, rewrite it around what is known.

### Never
- Estimate, round up, or "use industry averages" and present the result as fact.
- Turn "helped" into "led", or "supported" into "owned".
- Add ranges ("20–30%") that the candidate did not give.

## CV structure

### Default section order
1. Name, headline, contact line (email, phone, city, LinkedIn, portfolio)
2. Professional summary
3. Key skills (grouped)
4. Professional experience (reverse chronological)
5. Projects (optional: use for portfolio work, career changers or early careers)
6. Education
7. Certifications

Move Education above Experience only for recent graduates or academic roles.

### Length
- Under 10 years of experience: aim for 1 page (US) or 1 to 2 pages (UK, Ireland, Europe).
- 10 or more years: 2 pages. More than 2 only for academic or senior executive CVs.
- Roughly 450 to 900 words for most professional CVs.

### Experience entries
Each entry has the title, then the employer and location, then the dates on the right. Use one consistent date format (e.g. "2021 - Present" or "Mar 2021 - Present").

### Formatting for parsers
- One column, standard headings, plain bullets.
- Contact details in the body, not in a header or footer.
- No photos, graphics, tables for layout, or skill-rating bars.
- One clean sans-serif font at 10 to 11 pt body size.

### Things to leave out
"References available on request", full street addresses, date of birth, marital status, a photo (in the UK, Ireland and US), and an objective statement (the summary replaces it).
