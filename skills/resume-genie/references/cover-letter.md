# Cover letter

A short, specific letter that sounds like a capable person.

## Cover letter

### Shape (250 to 350 words, 3 or 4 paragraphs)
1. **Hook.** Open with the candidate's most relevant result or a specific connection to the company's work. Name the role. Never open with "I am writing to apply for…" or "I am excited to…".
2. **Proof.** Two or three pieces of evidence mapped to what the advert asks for most. Concrete beats adjectives: what they did, at what scale, with what result.
3. **The gap, handled.** If there is an obvious gap, name it briefly and show the nearest evidence or a plan. Honesty here builds trust. If there's no significant gap, use this paragraph for why this company and this role in particular.
4. **Close.** One or two sentences: what they would like to discuss, and thanks. No grovelling.

### Voice
- Plain, confident and warm. Write as the candidate would speak in a good interview.
- Short sentences. Active voice. Avoid "passionate", "dynamic", "synergy", "leverage" and "I believe I would be a great fit".
- Mirror two or three of the advert's key terms naturally. Don't stuff them in.

### Greeting
Use the hiring manager's name only if the advert gives it. Otherwise use "Dear Hiring Manager," (UK and Ireland) or "Dear Hiring Team," as a neutral option.

### Notes for the candidate
Finish by listing anything they should personalise or check before sending: a name to find, a claim to confirm, or a company detail to verify.
