# Career tracks

Apply the one track that fits the target role on top of the general CV rules.

## Track: finance transformation and GBS

Written from experience of finance transformation programmes: what hiring managers in Global Business Services, finance operations and ERP delivery look for.

- **Use the process vocabulary exactly.** P2P / procure-to-pay, O2C / order-to-cash, R2R / record-to-report, FP&A, month-end close, intercompany, treasury, tax. Recruiters search for both the acronym and the full name, so use both once.
- **Name the systems and modules.** SAP S/4HANA (FI/CO, MM), Oracle Fusion, Workday Financials, Coupa, Ariba, BlackLine, OneStream, Power BI. "ERP experience" alone carries no weight.
- **Show the transformation arc.** Assessment, then target operating model, then business case, then design, then build or test, then hypercare, then benefits realised. Make clear which stages the candidate owned.
- **Finance KPIs that land:** days to close, cost per invoice, straight-through processing or touchless rate, first-time-right, DPO and DSO, working capital released, FTE capacity freed, audit findings closed, SLA attainment.
- **Scale signals:** the number of countries, entities and legal entities, invoices or transactions a month, programme budget, and the size of the team or vendors managed.
- **Governance and control.** SOX and internal controls, segregation of duties, policy compliance and audit readiness. These matter as much as speed.
- **AI and automation roles.** Separate the use case (invoice data extraction, exception triage, cash application matching) from the technology (IDP, RPA, ML, generative AI), and show adoption and control, not just the build.
- **Product owner pivots.** Translate programme work into product terms where it's true: backlog of requirements, prioritised releases, user acceptance as acceptance criteria, adoption KPIs.

## Track: tech, product and data

- **The skills section is a search index.** Group it as Languages / Frameworks / Cloud and infrastructure / Data / Practices. List only what the candidate has used in real work or substantial projects.
- **Show the stack in context.** "Built a Python and FastAPI service on AWS Lambda handling [N] requests a day" is better than a bare tool list.
- **Engineers:** emphasise systems built, scale (users, requests, data volume), reliability (uptime, incidents reduced), performance (latency, cost), and engineering practices (CI/CD, testing, code review).
- **Product managers and owners:** emphasise outcomes over output. Cover the problem found, how it was prioritised, what shipped, and the metric that moved (adoption, retention, conversion, time saved). Use product language where it's true: backlog, discovery, roadmap, user stories, experiments.
- **Data roles:** cover the question asked, the method, the decision it changed and the business impact. Name the tools (SQL, Python, dbt, Power BI, Tableau).
- **Links matter.** Include GitHub, a portfolio or published work in the contact line when the candidate has them.
- **Projects section.** Include it for side projects, hackathons and open source, especially when there's little relevant job history.
- Avoid buzzword piles ("AI/ML/Blockchain/Cloud-native"). Every technology listed should be defensible in a technical interview.

## Track: senior leadership and executive

- **Lead with scope.** For each role, state the P&L or budget, headcount, geographies and who they reported to. Put this in a single italic line under the title, if the CV supports it.
- **Strategy, then execution.** Each role should show the strategic call they made and the measurable result for the organisation: growth, margin, transformation delivered, risk reduced, capability built.
- **Fewer, bigger bullets.** Use 3 to 5 per role. Each should be a business outcome, not an activity.
- **Board and stakeholder level.** Cover board reporting, investor or regulator engagement, M&A and integration, and change leadership across functions.
- **Summary as positioning.** 3 or 4 lines that say what kind of leader they are and the type of challenge they're hired for ("turnaround", "scale-up", "transformation").
- **Two pages.** Roles older than about 15 years can be listed as one line each under "Earlier career".
- **Tone.** Measured and factual. Executives lose credibility with hype.

## Track: career change

- **Translate, don't hide.** Keep the real titles and employers, but describe the work in the target field's language where that is honest. A teacher "designed curriculum for 120 learners" becomes "designed learning programmes for 120 users, measured outcomes and iterated each term".
- **Find transferable themes.** Look for stakeholder management, analysis, process improvement, project delivery, training others, budget responsibility and customer handling. Map each to the advert's requirements.
- **Bridge evidence first.** Put courses, certifications, side projects, volunteering or internal secondments in the new field near the top, in a Projects or "Relevant experience" section.
- **Summary as narrative.** One line on the past, one line on what carries over, one line on the new direction and the proof of commitment.
- **Skills section.** Use target-field terms the candidate genuinely has. Leave old-field jargon off unless it's relevant.
- **Cover letter.** It matters more for career changers. Address the switch directly and confidently.

## Track: academic

- **Different rules.** Academic CVs are comprehensive, not brief. Length grows with the career, and nothing is cut to fit two pages.
- **Order.** Education (with thesis title and supervisor), appointments, publications, grants and funding, teaching, supervision, conferences and invited talks, service (committees, reviewing), awards, then skills.
- **Publications.** Use one consistent citation style. Bold the candidate's name. Separate peer-reviewed articles, conference papers, book chapters and preprints. Mark them "under review" or "in press" honestly.
- **Grants.** Give the funder, title, role (PI or Co-I), amount and years.
- **Teaching.** List the modules, the level, the number of students and the candidate's role (lecturer, tutor, module lead).
- **Industry roles.** For research roles in industry, add a 3 to 4 line research summary at the top, and lead with methods and impact rather than a full publication list.

## Track: creative and design

- **The portfolio does the visual work.** The CV stays clean and parseable, so put the portfolio link in the contact line and the headline.
- **Show process and outcomes.** Cover the brief or problem, the research or insight, the creative decision, and the result (engagement, conversion, awards, client retention).
- **Name tools and media.** Figma, Adobe CC, motion, 3D, copywriting, brand systems. Group them in the skills section.
- **Clients and brands.** Name them where allowed. Recognisable names are evidence.
- **Collaboration.** Show work with product, engineering and marketing. Creative roles are judged on how well people work with others too.
- **Visual flair.** A small accent colour or a distinctive font for the name is fine. Keep layouts one column for ATS, and offer a designed PDF separately.
