# Job analysis and ATS keywords

How Resume Genie reads a job advert, judges fit honestly, and picks the keywords an applicant tracking system will search for.

## Job match analysis

### Read the advert in layers
1. **Must-haves.** Look for words like "required", "essential", "you will have", minimum years and named qualifications. These are the screening criteria.
2. **Nice-to-haves.** "Desirable", "bonus", "ideally", "familiarity with". These decide between shortlisted people, not who gets shortlisted.
3. **The real job.** The "what you will do" list shows what the person will spend their weeks on. Weight requirements that appear here as well as in the requirements list.
4. **Signals.** Seniority (who they report to, budget or team size), the stage of the company, and the domain language they use.

### Judge each requirement against the CV
For each requirement, decide one of three things:
- **met**: the CV shows it directly, with a role, project or result you can point to.
- **partial**: adjacent or implied. For example, they ran requirements workshops but never held a Product Owner title.
- **missing**: nothing in the CV supports it.

Always quote or paraphrase the evidence. If there is none, say what is missing. Never mark something "met" because it is plausible for someone with that background.

### Scoring fit (0 to 100)
Start from the share of must-haves that are met (partial counts as half), then adjust:
- Lower it sharply if a hard gate is missing: a licence, a security clearance, a degree the advert insists on, or a required language.
- Raise it modestly for strong evidence on the "real job" tasks, or for directly relevant domain experience.
- Seniority mismatch in either direction costs points.

Verdicts: **strong-apply** (85+), **apply** (65–84), **stretch** (45–64, worth it with a strong cover letter), **skip** (below 45, or a hard gate is missing).

### Gaps
A gap is only useful with advice attached. For each one, say how to handle it: surface hidden evidence ("if you did X, say so in these words"), address it head-on in the cover letter, or plan to close it (a course, a certification, a side project).

### Red flags in the advert
Point these out kindly, not cynically: three jobs' worth of duties in one role, a salary far out of line with the seniority, vague duties with long hours, "rockstar" or "family" language, or very high turnover signals.

### Strategy
Finish with 4 to 6 concrete, ordered tailoring moves specific to this CV and this job. "Highlight leadership" is too vague. "Move the S/4HANA migration bullet to the top of the 2019 role and name the modules" is useful.

## ATS keywords

### How screening really works
Most applicant tracking systems do not "reject" CVs on their own. Recruiters filter and search the database, and a CV that lacks the advert's literal terms simply never comes up. So the goal is to use **the advert's own phrases** wherever the candidate's real experience supports them.

### Choosing keywords
- Take 15 to 30 terms, using the advert's exact wording: "Procure-to-Pay", not "purchasing".
- Prefer nouns and noun phrases: tools, methods, domains, certifications and job titles. Skip soft fluff like "team player" unless the advert repeats it.
- Mark each keyword **required** if it appears in the must-have section or more than once in the duties. Otherwise mark it **preferred**.
- Add synonyms that a fair matcher should accept: acronyms and their expansions (P2P / procure-to-pay / purchase-to-pay), spelling variants (optimise / optimize), and closely equivalent tool names.
- Include the job title itself. Recruiters search for it first.

### Placement that helps
1. The headline under the name (mirror the target title where it is honest).
2. The summary (3 to 5 of the most important terms).
3. The skills section (plain words, grouped, no star ratings or progress bars).
4. Inside achievement bullets. This is where keywords carry the most weight with human readers.

### Formats that break parsers
Avoid text inside images, multi-column layouts built from tables, headers and footers that hold contact details, icons used instead of words, and unusual section names ("My journey" instead of "Experience"). Use standard headings: Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Certifications.

### Keep it honest
Never add a keyword the candidate cannot talk about in an interview. A missing must-have is a gap to report, not a word to slip in.
